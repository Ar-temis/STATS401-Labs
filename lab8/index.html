Lab 8: Web Text Data and Visualization: Exploring a Large Bulletin

Back to home

The 400-page DKU undergraduate bulletin, split into 1,334 passages and shown two ways: where each passage sits in the bulletin's formal structure, and where it sits in a semantic space built from sentence embeddings. The semantic map and the Topic x Section matrix are linked, so a selection in one is highlighted in the other.

1. Bulletin / Corpus Description

  • Document: Bulletin of Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction 2021-2022, dated July 2021 (PDF created 2021-08-23), 400 pages.
  • Source: official DKU admissions site, dku-web-admissions…/V2021-22_DKU_UG_Bulletin.pdf, accessed 2026-09-27.
  • Structure: the PDF's own bookmark outline (1,142 entries, up to 5 levels deep) gives the hierarchy. chapter = Part (level 1), section = level 2, subsection = level 3. Deeper headings such as a single course title go in an extra heading column. Printed page numbers match PDF page numbers.
  • Passages: text lines were grouped into paragraphs using vertical gaps. Heading lines were matched to bookmarks and removed. Fragments under 30 words such as table rows, bullet items, "Prerequisite(s):" lines were merged with neighbours under the same heading. Paragraphs over 250 words were split at sentence boundaries.
  • Cleaning: the cover, committee page and table of contents, page-number footers, small-font footnotes, superscripts and repeated table headers were removed before splitting. That produced 1,484 raw passages. Dropping 132 duplicated passages (major-requirement tables repeated across tracks) and 18 fragments under 10 words (e.g. "Continuation of CHINESE 101A. Prerequisite(s): CHINESE 101A") left 1,334 passages.
  • Result: average passage length 75.7 words (median 69.5, range 11–241), in 12 chapters and 83 formal sections.

3. Semantic Topics

  • Embeddings: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (sentence-transformers), one 384-dimensional vector per passage, with normalize_embeddings=True.
  • Projection: UMAP with n_components=2, n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.15, metric="cosine", random_state=401.
  • Clustering: K-means on the original 384-d embeddings (not the 2-D UMAP coordinates), n_clusters=12, random_state=401. Silhouette scores were flat (0.09–0.10) for k = 6–16, so k was chosen by reading clusters. At k = 8 all policy text fell into two or three mixed clusters. At k = 12 it separates into academic standing, grading/credit, transfer/study away, and institutional topics.
  • Labels: written by hand after reading the five passages closest to each cluster centre and the cluster's top TF-IDF terms (TF-IDF computed with one document per cluster).

5. Semantic Findings

Passage ids (e.g. p0203) can be typed into the search box to find the passage on the map.

1. What are the major semantic topics?

The twelve topics fall into two groups that also sit apart on the map. Seven are academic-content topics made almost entirely of Part 10 course descriptions and major requirement lists: Arts, Media & Humanities (197), Math, Physical Sciences & Computing (183), Environment, Economics & Health (148), History, Asia & Global Cultures (130), Major Structure & Electives (114), Politics, Policy & Governance (110) and Language Learning (66). The other five are institutional topics: Grading, Credit Rules & Calendar (109), DKU Institution & Services (75), Academic Standing & Withdrawal (71), Sports, Wellness & Student Support (66), and Transfer Credit, Study Away & Costs (65). On the map the content topics form large discipline-like regions. Academic Standing, Transfer Credit and DKU Institution are the tightest groups. Grading, Credit Rules & Calendar is the most scattered, because it mixes calendar dates, credit arithmetic and AP credit entries.

2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

The institutional topics cross the bulletin's formal structure. The content topics do not. DKU Institution & Services occurs in 44 sections across 10 of the 12 Parts: mission statements, office descriptions and contact-style passages appear everywhere. Grading, Credit Rules & Calendar covers 27 sections in 8 Parts. It holds all 16 Academic Calendar passages, 37 Part 6 policy passages and 21 "Credit for Advanced Placement…" course entries found inside Course Descriptions. Sports, Wellness & Student Support (24 sections) and Academic Standing & Withdrawal (22 sections) come next. By contrast, Math, Physical Sciences & Computing and Politics, Policy & Governance appear in only 2 sections (Majors and Course Descriptions). In the matrix, the institutional columns are sparse but tall, and the content columns are concentrated in the last two rows.

3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

Measured by topic entropy (the bar at the right of each matrix row) and by mean cosine distance from the section's own centroid, Course Descriptions is the most diverse (10 topics, 2.98 bits, distance 0.58), followed by Majors (8 topics, 2.63 bits, 0.50). Both are catalogues covering every discipline. Among policy sections, Degree Requirements is the most diverse (6 topics, 2.31 bits). It mixes the Common Core, language rules, Signature Work and credit arithmetic. Entrance Credit and Placement (4 topics, 1.81 bits) and Changes in Status (4 topics, 1.62 bits) follow. The most uniform sections are Academic Calendar 2021-22 (all 16 passages in one topic), Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere (0.50 bits) and Refund (0.54 bits).

4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

The strongest cross-section pairs are policies restated in more than one place.

  1. Grading, CR/NC system: study-abroad courses cannot be taken CR/NC and Study Away: grades earned at Duke: 0.96
  2. Entrance Credit, Prematriculation Credit and Transfer of Work, Work Taken During High School: 0.92
  3. Course Load, Medical Withdrawal from a Course and Changes in Status, Leave of Absence: 0.90
  4. International Student Services and Host Family Program: 0.87

5. Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section?

The detail panel shows each passage's cosine similarity to its section's centroid ("typicality"). Low values flag outliers. In the policy chapters: p0017 (Partners, Kunshan) is about Kunqu opera (typicality 0.48). It falls in History & Global Cultures, and its nearest neighbours are a Part 9 Arts and Culture passage and four humanities course descriptions. p0312, the study-away insurance rule (0.48), has five nearest neighbours that are all cost passages from Part 5 (Estimated Expenses, Tuition and Fees). Semantically it is a financial passage filed under Study Away. In Course Descriptions, the 21 "Credit for Advanced Placement…" entries are placed in Grading & Credit Rules, away from the course regions, because they are credit rules rather than courses. Low typicality is not always a real outlier. p0059 (Signature Work, 0.44) has only Degree Requirements passages as neighbours, but it sits at the edge of a diverse section and K-means placed it in the Student Support topic.

6. Does credit, graduation, registration or academic integrity occur in one region or several?

Credit is everywhere: 163 passages in all 12 topics and 30 sections. It is still concentrated in two regions: Grading, Credit Rules & Calendar (70) and Transfer Credit, Study Away & Costs (35). Most other matches are course descriptions ("may be repeated for credit"). Graduation (32 passages, 17 sections) appears only in the 4 institutional topics: Grading/Credit (12), Institution (7), Academic Standing (7) and Transfer (6).
Registration matches just 9 passages in 7 sections, spread over 4 topics. Even the two matches inside the Registration section are split: unpaid fines blocking registration (p0147) is in Academic Standing, and advising before registration (p0146) is in Student Support.
The phrase academic integrity appears in only 3 passages, each in a different topic. The Academic Integrity section's own five passages are split four ways: the Community Standard pledge with Institution, disciplinary procedure with Academic Standing, violations abroad with Study Away, and the confidential UARB review with Student Support.

6. Design Description

Embeddings and UMAP. Each of the 1,334 cleaned passages was encoded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 into a normalized 384-d vector, without removing stop words. UMAP (cosine metric, 15 neighbours, min_dist 0.15, seed 401) projects the vectors to 2-D for display only. Cosine similarity in the full space is used for the 5 nearest neighbours.

Topics. K-means (k = 12) clusters the full embeddings, not the UMAP coordinates. I labelled each cluster after reading its five most central passages and its top cluster-level TF-IDF terms. TF-IDF is used to interpret clusters, while the embeddings organize passages.

Encodings. Position encodes semantic similarity, colour encodes semantic topic (switchable to bulletin chapter, to compare formal and semantic structure on the same layout), and circle area encodes passage length. The matrix uses a sequential blue scale for passage count or row share, with a topic-entropy bar per row for section diversity. Filtered-out passages fade to 6% opacity instead of disappearing, so context remains.

Coordination. Search, the section/topic dropdowns, topic-table rows, legend entries and matrix cells all write to one shared filter state that both views read. Clicking a matrix cell filters the map to that section x topic. Selecting a point outlines its cell in the matrix and scrolls to it, and draws lines to its 5 nearest neighbours, which are listed with their sections in the detail panel. A search also recounts the matrix, so one query shows both where a concept lives in the bulletin and which semantic regions it belongs to.
