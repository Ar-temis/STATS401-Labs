1. Dataset Description

  • Hierarchy: World -> 6 continents -> 15 areas -> 27 countries. The flat CSV was converted to nested JSON with convert_hierarchy.py.
  • Leaf attributes: gdp_billion_usd and gdp_status.

2. How to Read the Treemaps

Every colored cell is a country. Cell area is proportional to GDP and cell color (plus the ▲ ▬ ▼ glyph before the name) is the GDP status. Countries are nested inside grey area boxes, which are nested inside outlined continent boxes, each with a header label. Hover a country for its details, or hover a continent or area header for that group's total GDP and status breakdown. Labels are hidden where they would not fit.

Voice commands (Lab 10)

Click Start voice and allow microphone access, then speak in English. Say a country, area, continent or status to highlight it in both treemaps, e.g. "highlight Asia", "show China and India", "highlight East Asia" or "highlight decreasing". Say "reset" or "clear" to remove the highlighting. Without voice, click a country, an area/continent header, or a status in the legend to highlight it, and use the Reset button.

Voice is off. The microphone is only used after you click Start voice.

3. Treemap A: Squarify

d3.treemapSquarify chooses, at every level, the split that keeps rectangles as close to squares as possible.

4. Treemap B: Slice-and-dice

d3.treemapSliceDice alternates direction by depth: continents are vertical columns, areas are horizontal rows inside each column, and countries are columns again inside each row.

5. Design Description

GDP is the main quantity, so it takes the channel a treemap does best: area. GDP status can increase, decrease, or not change at all, so it maps to hue with the conventional green, red, grey meanings, and a ▲ ▼ ▬ glyph in each label repeats the status so it survives colorblindness problem. The hierarchy is shown by containment rather than color: an outlined, headed box for each continent, a grey headed box for each area, and padding between levels. Hue is deliberately not spent on continent; the headers and the tooltip answer "where is this?" instead.

Squarify packs each group into compact, near-square cells, so even Kenya and New Zealand are visible and most labels fit, but the order of cells inside a group is hard to follow. Slice-and-dice keeps a strict, predictable order (largest continent on the left, largest area at the top of each column, largest country at the left of each row), and cells in one row compare by width alone. But it squeezes Africa and Oceania into columns a few pixels wide with unlabelled countries, and such slivers convey area poorly.

Voice (Lab 10). The task voice makes easier is locating a named item: finding "Vietnam" or "Southern Europe" by eye means scanning two dense treemaps, but saying its name highlights it in both at once, which also makes Squarify and Slice-and-dice easy to compare for the same group. Highlighting dims every other country and outlines the match, so the position and relative area of the selection stay in context. The status line echoes what was heard and what was matched, so recognition errors are visible. The longest matching name wins ("East Asia" over "Asia"), several names can be combined, and click plus the Reset button remain as a fallback.
