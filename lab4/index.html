Lab 4: Cleaning Web Data for Visualization

Sentiment of 2,000 tweets from the Trump Twitter Archive, estimated with a RoBERTa model and broken down by year and by the device each tweet was sent from.

  • Dataset: Trump Twitter Archive — 56,571 tweets (2009–2021) with text, device, likes, retweets, and timestamp
  • Sample: 2,000 tweets drawn at random (seed 401) from the tweets left after cleaning
  • Sentiment: cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest
  • Script: lab4/clean_tweets.py

Pick a device to see how that account's sentiment mix differs. Hover a bar or a point for the underlying counts.

Analysis

The dataset is the Trump Twitter Dataset: 56,571 tweets from 2009–2021 with text, device, likes, retweets, and a timestamp. Cleaning removed 453 duplicated texts, 9,797 retweets (quoted words are not the account's own writing), and 1,269 tweets whose text was nothing but a link. HTML entities such as &amp; were unescaped, engagement counts coerced to numbers, timestamps parsed into year/hour/weekday, and the free-text device string grouped into iPhone, Android, Web, and Other. Years before 2012 hold too few tweets to sample from, so the chart covers 2012–2020. From what remained, 2,000 tweets were sampled (seed 401) and scored with cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest; sentiment_score is P(positive) − P(negative). The bars encode each year's sentiment mix, the black line its mean score. Two patterns stand out: negativity climbs from 19% of tweets in 2015 to 44% in 2019, and the Android tweets read as far more positive (mean 0.27, 28% negative) than the iPhone ones (0.09, 40%) — though the two devices barely overlap in time, so era and device are hard to separate.

The sentiment values here are model-generated estimates, not ground-truth labels. RoBERTa reads context well but still misjudges sarcasm, irony, and slang, so the yearly shares should be read as a rough signal, not a measurement.
