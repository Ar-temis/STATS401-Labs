Lab 7: Animated Temporal Commercial Network

Back to home

Sixty days of commercial transactions among twelve companies, drawn as a force-directed network that is redrawn one day at a time. Press Play to watch relationships appear and disappear, or drag the slider (or click a bar in the overview) to inspect any single day.

1. Dataset Description

  • Companies: 12 fabricated companies, each with a sector (7 sectors) and a region (Asia, Europe, North America; 4 companies each).
  • Transactions: 367 daily records from 2026-01-01 (day 1) to 2026-03-01 (day 60), each linking two companies with an amount_usd ($7.3k–$33.9k), a transaction_type (goods, shipping, components, materials, services) and a transaction_count (1–5). Links are treated as undirected; 27 distinct company pairs ever transact.

3. Design Description

Company attributes. Sector is the node's hue and region is its shape, so both attributes are readable at any size. The dynamic property is today's transaction volume, mapped to node area. A company that transacts nothing today shrinks to a small, faded mark with a grey outline, while an active company grows and gets a dark outline. Tooltips add the exact volume, today's partners and the company's 60-day totals.

Link attributes. Link color is the transaction type and link width is the amount in USD; the tooltip adds the transaction count and whether the pair is within one region or crosses two. Links are joined by an undirected pair key, so a relationship that appears fades in and briefly flashes to full opacity, a relationship that persists keeps its line (only width and color transition), and a relationship that disappears fades out.

Mental map. Each frame only swaps the link force's links, refreshes collision radii and restarts at alpha(0.3). Because the day-to-day link sets change almost completely (most pairs trade every second or third day), a link force alone would fling nodes around, so every company also has a fixed home position inside its region zone and a forceX/forceY pair that is stronger than the link force pulls it back there. Nodes therefore lean toward the day's partners but drift only a few tens of pixels between days, and the region zones double as a visual answer to the cross-regional question. The overview bar chart, the slider, the ◀ ▶ step buttons and the pause button all let the viewer stop at, and compare, specific days without relying on memory of previous frames.

4. Temporal Network Questions

1. How does overall connectivity change across the 60 days?

Connectivity is strongly periodic at the day scale and grows in steps at the phase scale. The ten "core" pairs rotate on a three-day cycle (e.g. Cedar–Evergreen, Granite–Harbor and Ion–Lumen every third day from day 1), so the daily link count zig-zags between 3 and 8 for the first 20 days. The overview shows three distinct levels: days 1–20 average 5.5 links and about $77k per day, days 21–40 average 5.6 links but about $97k per day, and days 41–60 jump to 7.3 links and about $144k per day, with the densest days (45, 51, 57) reaching 10 links and all 12 companies active. The network is therefore busiest and most connected in the final third.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active?

Fusion Electronics (Asia, Technology) is the hub of the middle phase: from day 21 it gains new partners Harbor, Lumen and Juniper on top of Apex, Delta and Granite, reaching six distinct partners and about $1.05M of volume in days 21–40, two to three times its volume in either other phase. In the final phase the centre of gravity moves to North America: Ion Systems goes from one partner (days 21–40) to five (Lumen, Juniper, Delta, Apex, Fusion) and about $1.1M, and Cedar Retail goes from two partners to five (Evergreen, BlueRiver, Granite, Keystone, Harbor) and about $0.9M. Both nodes are visibly the largest marks in most frames after day 41. Evergreen Foods stays peripheral throughout, trading only with Cedar until Lumen appears on day 42.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of separate clusters?

Yes. On the "day 1, 4, 7, …" rotation in the first 20 days the network is just three disconnected pairs (Cedar–Evergreen, Granite–Harbor, Ion–Lumen), and days 3, 9 and similar are four disconnected pairs. In days 21–40 the graph is a large Fusion-centred component plus one or two isolated pairs, and days 24, 30 and 36 briefly merge into a single six-company component. In the final phase two clusters alternate: an Ion-centred group (Ion, Delta, Juniper, Lumen) and a Cedar-centred group (Cedar, Granite, Keystone, plus BlueRiver or Evergreen) on odd days, and an eight-company component on days 42, 48, 54 and 60. The network is never fully connected on any single day.

4. Which relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger?

Ten pairs are permanent, recurring every third day across all 60 days (Apex–Fusion, Apex–BlueRiver, Delta–Fusion, Harbor–Juniper, Granite–Keystone, Cedar–Evergreen, Ion–Lumen, BlueRiver–Cedar, Juniper–Lumen, Ion–Juniper). Three pairs exist only in days 1–20 and disappear on day 20 (BlueRiver–Delta, Cedar–Fusion, Harbor–Keystone). On day 21 Fusion's new links to Harbor, Lumen and Juniper appear, and Delta–Keystone appears on day 24; all of these, plus Granite–Harbor and Fusion–Granite, disappear after day 40. Day 41–42 brings a wave of eight new pairs, including Cedar–Granite, Cedar–Keystone, Delta–Ion, Apex–Ion, Fusion–Ion and Evergreen–Lumen, none of which existed before. Links also become stronger: the average link amount rises from about $14k in phase one to about $20k in phase three, which is visible as thicker lines in the final third.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional?

More cross-regional. Cross-region links make up 64% of links (61% of value) in days 1–20, 63% (69% of value) in days 21–40, and 72% (80% of value) in days 41–60. On the map, the early days show several links that stay inside a region zone (Cedar–Evergreen and Ion–Lumen in North America, Granite–Harbor in Europe, Apex–Fusion in Asia), while the final phase adds mostly zone-crossing links: Ion's new partners are in Europe (Delta) and Asia (Apex, Fusion), and Cedar's new partners are in Europe (Granite, Harbor) and Asia (Keystone). The only new within-region link late in the period is Evergreen–Lumen.
